Research & Clinical

Breakthrough: Word Recognition and Usage Hours Predict Cochlear Implant Outcomes

Emerging Research Grants scientist Nicole T. Jiam, M.D., has tracked word recognition scores alongside daily processor usage hours at specific time-points post-activation, identifying word recognition test scores and usage time as useful for predicting future outcomes. This research offers clinicians an evidence-based roadmap for counseling CI candidates about realistic expectations.

Hearing Health Foundation

Drug-Releasing Electrode Protects Residual Hearing During Cochlear Implantation

A porous parylene cochlear-implant electrode loaded with dexamethasone provided sustained local drug delivery in preclinical models. This advance could preserve valuable residual hearing during implantation, particularly important for hybrid device candidates.

Medical Xpress

FDA Grants De Novo Approval to Lenire Tinnitus Treatment Device

The FDA's De Novo approval is based on the success of Lenire's third large-scale clinical trial…over the entire trial, 79.4% of the patients experienced a clinically significant improvement…and 88.6% responded that they would recommend Lenire as a tinnitus treatment. Patients who were at least moderately bothered by Tinnitus were more likely to achieve a clinically meaningful improvement using Lenire's bimodal sound and tongue stimulation than sound therapy alone.

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy Remains Gold Standard for Tinnitus Distress

Tinnitus distress is highly treatable, particularly through CBT and integrated care pathways. Best-practice care in 2026 is multimodal and personalized: treat reversible ear conditions and hearing loss first, deploy CBT for distress, fit hearing aids or structured sound therapy for auditory input restoration.

The Minimum Speech Test Battery–Version 3, released in 2024, can help determine cochlear implant candidacy and assess cochlear implant performance. The authors found that in the 2 years since its development, this test battery has led to increased consistency in both evaluation and follow-up care.
