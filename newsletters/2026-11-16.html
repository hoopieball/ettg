Research & Clinical

Phase 3 ELEVATE Trial Completes Enrollment for Totally Implantable Cochlear Implants

A Phase 3 clinical trial across 11 national sites has concluded enrollment to evaluate the safety and performance of an investigational totally implantable cochlear implant designed without external components. Investigators hope the technology may eventually encourage more people to consider cochlear implantation, as fully implantable technology may help expand access to hearing restoration for patients reluctant to pursue traditional implants.

MD+DI Online

Light-Based Cochlear Implants Show Superior Neural Precision in Gerbil Study

Today's cochlear implants use electrical impulses to stimulate the auditory nerve, but researchers comparing light-based signals with those elicited by sounds and electrical implants demonstrate that optical cochlear implants represent another important step toward developing this technology. Early-stage optogenetic approaches show promise for enhanced frequency specificity.

Medical Xpress

Duration of Deafness Should Not Exclude Candidates from Cochlear Implants

A study published November 14 investigating 56 adult patients with cochlear implant after unilateral sudden sensorineural hearing loss found that duration of deafness should not be an exclusion criterion for cochlear implantation in SSNHL patients. This finding may broaden candidacy criteria for single-sided deafness.

European Archives of Otolaryngology

Pediatric Cochlear Implants Using Round Window Approach Show Preserved Vestibular Function

A prospective study of 20 pediatric cochlear implant recipients found that round window approach implantation was associated with early postoperative changes in VEMP-based measures of otolithic function, while semicircular canal function remained largely preserved, supporting routine use of objective vestibular assessment in pediatric CI candidates.

Journal of International Advanced Otology

Arizona Clinics Deploy Advanced Frequency Mapping for Tinnitus Management

Sound Relief Tinnitus & Hearing Center in Mesa and Scottsdale is rolling out new technology to target and manage tinnitus using 67 different frequencies, with experts noting that understanding tinnitus and exploring new neuroplasticity treatments can help patients avoid simply living with the condition. The approach aligns with emerging evidence supporting customized sound therapy.

Clinical & Community

New Narrative Review Emphasizes Spatial Processing Disorder Diagnosis and Rehabilitation

Central auditory processing disorder, particularly spatial processing disorder (a subtype), impairs auditory stream segregation and can significantly affect daily functioning. Rehabilitation programs such as LiSN & Learn have demonstrated significant improvements in binaural processing abilities and speech understanding in noise. Both free- and headphone-based tests, including the LiSN-S test, are effective for evaluating spatial auditory processing.

PubMed

Critical Review Highlights Diagnostic Ambiguity in Pediatric Auditory Processing Disorder

Despite decades of international research, there remains no consensus on how APD in children should be defined or diagnosed, with children referred for APD assessment often presenting heterogeneous profiles extending beyond auditory processing to include co-occurring language, attention, and learning difficulties. This variability underscores the need for interdisciplinary assessment frameworks.
