Bimodal Neuromodulation Shows Superior Results for Moderate-to-Severe Tinnitus

In participants with moderate or more severe tinnitus, there is a clinically superior performance of bimodal treatment (58.6%) combining sound therapy with electrical tongue stimulation compared to sound therapy alone (43.2%). These positive outcomes led to FDA De Novo approval of the Lenire device for tinnitus treatment.

Nature Communications

Novel Sound Therapy for Tinnitus Shows Promise Via Smartphone Delivery

A pioneering Newcastle University-led study unveiled a new sound therapy that reduces tinnitus loudness with potential online delivery via smartphones. The research works by introducing a sound modulation technique to disrupt patterns of activity in the brain's sound processing regions. With the study conducted entirely online, researchers believe the treatment has potential for global delivery at minimal cost, and plan to explore development into a user-friendly software platform for global distribution.

Multimodal Approach Improves Tinnitus Outcomes in 2026 Clinical Practice

Best-practice care in 2026 is multimodal and personalized: treat reversible ear conditions and hearing loss first, deploy CBT for distress, fit hearing aids or structured sound therapy for auditory input restoration, consider bimodal neuromodulation for eligible, persistent cases, and reserve pharmacologic or invasive options for trials.

Evidence-Based Care Review

APD Diagnosis Requires Multidisciplinary Assessment; Early Intervention Critical

Diagnosis of APD is complex and a multidisciplinary team is required to accurately identify the condition. Assessment may include thorough history, specialist AP behavioral tests, full audiometric testing, immittance testing, speech testing in quiet and in background noise, otoacoustic emissions, neurological auditory brainstem response, and speech and language assessment. Treatment typically focuses on three primary areas: changing the communication environment, learning to use higher-order skills to help compensate for the deficits, and remediation of the auditory deficit itself.
