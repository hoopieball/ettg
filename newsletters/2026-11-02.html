Industry & Product News

WSA Cybersecurity Incident Disrupts Widex and Signia Services

WS Audiology (WSA), the parent company of Widex and Signia, is investigating a cybersecurity incident after unauthorized parties accessed parts of its IT infrastructure. For WSA customers, the immediate impact involved app controls, fitting tools, and remote support rather than a confirmed failure of hearing aid hardware. Services are being restored incrementally.

Hearing Review

Ontario Moves to Permit Over-the-Counter Hearing Aids

The Ford government's planned legislative change, expected to be formalized in the province's fall economic update, would allow adults with mild to moderate hearing loss to purchase OTC hearing aids without a prescription. This regulatory shift could expand access to affordable hearing technology in Canada's largest province.

Hearing Review

Samsung Galaxy Buds Hearing Aid Feature Coming Q4 2026

The FDA cleared Samsung's Hearing Aid Feature in July 2026, and Galaxy Buds3 Pro and Buds4 Pro are scheduled to get the feature in the U.S. in Q4 2026. Users can take a five-minute hearing test, generate an audiogram, and have the earbuds fitted according to the NAL-NL2 prescription formula used in clinical hearing-aid fitting.

Lenire Added to AAO Clinical Practice Guidelines for Tinnitus

The American Academy of Otolaryngology has included Lenire as a treatment option in the Academy's new Clinical Practice Guidelines for Tinnitus, the first new treatment added since establishment of the Academy's tinnitus guidelines. This marks an important clinical validation for bimodal neuromodulation therapy.

Research & Clinical

Optical Cochlear Implants Show Promise in Preclinical Research

Research combined light-sensitive proteins with experimental optical cochlear implants, finding that optical stimulation produced more distinct patterns of auditory neural activity than electrical stimulation while requiring relatively small amounts of light energy. The findings suggest that advances in light-sensitive proteins and implant design may help address power limitations in optical cochlear implant research.

Hearing Health & Technology Matters

Cochlear's Fully Implantable System Reaches 1 Million Hours of Patient Experience

Cochlear has reached a new milestone in the development of its fully implantable hearing technology, with patients across the company's research programs now accumulating more than 1 million hours of experience with investigational devices. The research program is intended to build evidence around safety, performance, and real-world use while identifying features that matter most to recipients and clinicians.

Hearing Health & Technology Matters

NYU Research Explores Neuroplasticity to Enhance Cochlear Implant Outcomes

Neuroscientists and a cochlear implant surgeon at NYU Langone Health are harnessing neuroplasticity—the brain's capacity to reorganize in response to new experiences—to help people interpret sound more quickly and effectively. A clinical test at NYU's Cochlear Implant Center is evaluating a modified implant that repurposes an electrode to stimulate the vagus nerve.

2026 Tinnitus Guidelines Recommend CBT, Hearing Aids, and Bimodal Neuromodulation

The 2026 tinnitus guideline says everyone with tinnitus needs a full hearing evaluation and backs hearing aids, CBT, and bimodal neuromodulation. The guideline advises against routine medications, supplements, and magnetic stimulation for tinnitus itself.

Utah Hearing Aids

Serotonin Circuit Linked to Tinnitus in Recent Research

A specific brain circuit involving serotonin has been found to induce tinnitus-like effects; turning that circuit off was able to ameliorate tinnitus significantly. Clinicians are advised to help patients find a drug regimen that balances relief of psychiatric symptoms while minimizing tinnitus experience.

AI Chatbot Quality in Audiology Case Management Under Review

Recent research evaluates the quality of artificial intelligence chatbot responses in audiology case interpretation and management through blinded comparative study. As AI tools increasingly support clinical practice, quality assurance protocols remain essential for patient safety.
